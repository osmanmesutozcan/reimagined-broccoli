# Choice Overload

## Core idea

A large or difficult-to-compare set of options can leave people overwhelmed and reluctant to decide.

Source: [Choice Overload — Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com/choice-overload/).

## Apply in context

Help people establish criteria, narrow the set, and compare relevant differences. For example, let someone filter plans by their actual needs and compare a shortlist with consistent attributes.

## Use judgment

More choice can be valuable when people know what they want. Diagnose comparison difficulty before removing useful options. This lens concerns overload and confidence; Hick’s Law concerns decision time.

## Check the experience

Can someone explain why an option fits and complete the choice? Observe abandonment, repeated comparison, and later regret.
